Method & sources

What this combines

Architects usually run sun and wind in separate tools: Ladybug / Honeybee or ClimateStudio for sun, daylight and energy; SimScale, Orbital Stack or Autodesk Forma for wind. Forma is the closest to one place, with rapid machine-learning wind and solar side by side. Sun & Wind Lab puts both physics on one model, one timeline and one probe, and lets them talk: shade and wind feed one outdoor-comfort map, and the wind tunnel's façade pressures drive the energy model's air leakage.

Model

The context is every building within 300 m of the tower from NYC's official Building Footprints (NYC Open Data, photogrammetric footprints with surveyed roof heights), turned into prisms on Manhattan's grid, which runs 29° east of true north. Citigroup Center is lifted onto its base, as built. The Seagram Building itself is modelled by hand, because the city records it as one footprint at one height: its plan follows that footprint (slab, ten-storey bustle, five-storey wings and the light courts between them) and its heights follow the published section, with the slab rising 516 ft (157 m) behind the 90 ft plaza. The city footprint also georeferences the model. Setbacks above the base of neighbouring towers are not in the city data, so each neighbour rises at its footprint to its roof height.

Sun

Sun position uses NOAA's solar equations. Each month is represented by its standard mean day, hour by hour. Irradiance takes each month's average daily total from the climate data, shapes it across the day with a clear-sky curve, and splits beam from diffuse with the Erbs monthly correlation. Shading is exact ray casting against the model: one ray per sensor per sun position, and 128 cosine-weighted rays per sensor for sky view and ground reflection (albedo 0.2). Sensors sit every 3 m on the façades and the ground.

Wind tunnel

A three-dimensional lattice-Boltzmann solver (D3Q19, BGK collision with a Smagorinsky large-eddy closure) runs the flow around the block. The approach wind is a dense-urban power-law boundary layer (exponent 0.27, gradient height 450 m, after the AIJ guidelines). The ground is a rough partial-slip wall, the sides and top slip, and the buildings are rotated into the tunnel for each of 16 directions. The baseline uses 6 m cells; live re-runs in the browser use 6 or 8 m. Pedestrian speeds are read on the second cell above the ground and brought down to 2 m with the approach profile. Results are time-averaged after the flow has developed.

Wind comfort

Each point gets a speed ratio per direction (local mean wind ÷ airport 10 m wind). Combined with the wind rose and a Weibull fit per sector, that gives the share of hours above each Lawson LDDC threshold: sitting 4 m/s, standing 6, strolling 8, business walking 10, each at 5% of hours. Speeds are gust-equivalent means (the larger of the mean and (mean + 3σ) ÷ 1.85, from the tunnel's own fluctuations). Safety flags 15 m/s and 20 m/s exceeded more than 0.022% of the year (about two hours).

Outdoor comfort

"Feels like" is Steadman's apparent temperature with radiation: air temperature, humidity, the local wind from the tunnel, and the extra sun a person absorbs when the point is in direct sun (plus a little more where the sky is very open). Bands follow UTCI's thermal-stress labels. Comfort autonomy is the share of daytime hours (8 am–7 pm) in a season with no thermal stress, weighting every wind direction by the rose.

Energy

A monthly quasi-steady-state balance (ISO 13790) for the whole Seagram complex: conduction through glass, spandrels and roof; ventilation at 0.5 L/s·m² while occupied; solar gains from the façade sensors through the glass, net of fins and overhangs; office internal gains; and air leakage from wind and stack pressure, where the wind part uses the tunnel's façade-to-façade pressure difference for each direction. Heating plant efficiency and a chiller COP of 4.5 turn loads into site energy, plus lighting, plugs, fans and pumps. It is a screening model for comparing options, not a calibrated simulation of the real building.

Climate

The built-in typical year is assembled from monthly values: NOAA 1991–2020 normals for Central Park (temperature, humidity), NREL solar resource for New York City (daily global irradiation), and an approximate LaGuardia wind rose. Load an EPW weather file to replace all of it with measured hourly data; the rose, Weibull fits, temperatures and irradiance are rebuilt from the file.

Google 3D context

The Photoreal toggle streams Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles (Map Tiles API, with the site's key) into the same scene, turned onto Manhattan's grid, so the analysis sits in the real city. It is context only: sun and wind are computed on the model massing, shown as a yellow outline, because the tiles' terms do not allow extracting geometry from them. Nudge and snap controls line the two up; the offsets are saved in your browser.

Limits

Everything here is early-design screening, like Forma's rapid mode. 6 m cells resolve the tower and the streets but not doorways, trees or the plaza's pools; neighbours are boxes. Lawson results are sensitive to the wind climate. Before decisions, verify wind with a boundary-layer wind tunnel or detailed CFD, and energy with a calibrated EnergyPlus model.
